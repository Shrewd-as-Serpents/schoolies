## Primary-Source Control Statement

The primary source audited in this project is David L. Altheide's (1996) *Qualitative Media Analysis*, published by SAGE Publications, Inc. (DOI: [10.4135/9781412985536](https://doi.org/10.4135/9781412985536)). This audit accesses the text through its SAGE Research Methods (SRM) online chapter-download platform and cites it using SRM chapter titles plus the downloadable SRM PDF's internal page numbers (e.g., "Chapter 5, p. 11 of 12"), not the pagination of any print edition.

Earlier drafts of this protocol referenced a 2013 second edition (Altheide & Schneider) and a "2011 digital edition" label. Both were revised: the SAGE Research Methods record resolving from this DOI lists the publication year as 1996 and the sole author as David L. Altheide. The 2013 second edition is a materially different, co-authored, differently-paginated text (WorldCat OCLC 820790765) and is **not** the object audited here. No unverified print-page crosswalk is presented as a final citation anywhere in this project.

## Historical Scope of the Technology Audit

Because the audited work was originally published in 1996, its discussion of Gopher, VCR counters, CD-ROM systems, ZyIndex, and subscription-based research databases is analyzed as part of a specific mid-1990s research infrastructure. The audit does not claim that these tools were old-fashioned for their time. Its concern is that the method's evidentiary assumptions — such as the value of indexability, retrievability, standardization, and institutionally available archives — may still illuminate durable questions about what becomes researchable knowledge.

## Abstract & Operational Overview

This document presents a comprehensive, multi-tiered **Decolonial Methods Audit Protocol** designed to turn the decolonial analytical apparatus back onto the primary tools assigned to students for "objective" qualitative research. Focusing on David L. Altheide's *Qualitative Media Analysis* (1996) as a representative social product of Eurocentric research methodology, this protocol operationalizes Boaventura de Sousa Santos's *sociology of absences*, Ramón Grosfoguel's genealogy of epistemicides, Santiago Castro-Gómez's critique of zero-point hubris, and marcela polanco et al.'s framework of methodological extraction, storycide, and knowledge gentrification.

To resolve pagination and edition ambiguity between print monographs and digital **SAGE Research Methods (SRM)** chapter downloads, this protocol maps every audited passage using **Notebook PDF File Names** and **SRM Digital Chapter Page Numbers** only. No print-edition page number is used as a final citation.

## 2. Epistemic & Decolonial Theoretical Framework

### 2.1 Sociology of Absences & Monocultures of Knowledge
Boaventura de Sousa Santos (2014) posits that Westernized academic reason maintains its hegemony through a *sociology of absences*—a systematic process of producing "non-existence" by declaring alternative ways of knowing, temporalities, and social practices as ignorant, residual, inferior, local, or unproductive. Santos identifies five structural monocultures:
1. **Monoculture of Knowledge and Rigor**: Asserts that scientific/canonical knowledge is the sole criteria for truth, rendering all other epistemic forms ignorant.
2. **Monoculture of Linear Time**: Enforces a singular, progressive historical trajectory, labeling alternative temporalities as backward or obsolete.
3. **Monoculture of Naturalized Classification**: Organizes human and epistemic differences into racialized, gendered, and institutional hierarchies.
4. **Monoculture of the Dominant Scale**: Prioritizes universal/global abstractions over site-specific, vernacular realities.
5. **Monoculture of Capitalist Productivity**: Measures value exclusively through commodified output and efficiency, discarding non-extractive social relations.

### 2.2 Zero-Point Hubris (*La Hybris del Punto Zero*) & The "iCloud Researcher"
Santiago Castro-Gómez critiques the foundational posture of Eurocentric methodology: *la hybris del punto zero* (the hubris of the zero point). The zero-point stance creates the illusion of an unlocated, disembodied, and impartial observer who observes reality without being observed. As marcela polanco et al. (2020) argue, this produces the **"iCloud™ researcher"**—an entity that purports to exist "everywhere but nowhere," concealing racial privilege, geopolitical location, and institutional complicity behind disembodied academic passive phrasing ("the data indicates," "findings reveal").

### 2.3 Methodological Extraction, Storycide, and Knowledge Gentrification
Building on Nelson Maldonado-Torres, polanco et al. (2020) demonstrate that qualitative methods manuals treat "the Method" as an unquestioned technology of extraction. This extraction manifests in two primary violences:
* **Knowledge Gentrification ("Flip and Fix")**: The process wherein raw, emotional, or vernacular stories (*relatos*) told by marginalized participants are viewed as "deteriorated" or unscientific, prompting researchers to sanitize, translate, and "renovate" them into respectable academic prose for publication markets.
* **Storycide (*Historicidio*) & Knowledge Captivity**: The destruction or devaluation of autonomous stories. Stories are "vacuum-packed" (*empacadas al vacío*), coded, thematized, and "NVivo-ized." Story elements that do not conform to the researcher's pre-coded protocol are discarded or locked away in proprietary databases, resulting in epistemic muteness.

### 2.4 Trouillot's Four Moments of Silencing (Project Shorthand)
Michel-Rolph Trouillot (1995) identifies four moments at which silence can enter the production of history: the moment of *fact creation* (making sources), *fact assembly* (making archives), *fact retrieval* (making narratives), and *retrospective significance* (making history in the final instance). This project uses internal codes such as `FACTCREATION`, `FACTASSEMBLY`, `FACTRETRIEVAL`, and `RETROSPECTSIG` as project-specific shorthand for these four moments. These codes are **our labels**, not Trouillot's own terminology, and are used only to mark which of his four moments a given audit record's "Historical Lens" note is drawing an interpretive comparison to.

## 5. Reflexive Audit Memos & Qualitative Reliability Protocol

To satisfy **Jerome Kirk and Marc L. Miller's (1986)** qualitative reliability standards without falling into zero-point detachment, every audit record undergoes three explicit reliability checks before it can be promoted out of the verification queue:

1. **Quixotic Reliability Prevention**: Audits verify that monoculture tags are grounded in specific textual mechanisms rather than applied as formulaic, automated labels.
2. **Diachronic Reliability (14-Day Test/Re-Test)**: The primary auditor re-codes excerpts after a 14-day interval. Category boundaries (such as distinguishing `MONO_KNOWLEDGE` from `MONO_SCALE`) must remain stable across test sessions.
3. **Synchronic Reliability (Cross-Modal & Inter-Coder Checks)**: Textual findings are cross-checked against visual diagrams (e.g., Figure 3.2 ECA flowchart) and validated through secondary coder consensus.

As of this revision, none of the eight verification-queue records in Section 1 have completed all three checks; each record's `reliability_check` fields in `sage-audit-data-final.json` are marked `not_completed` until that work is logged. This status is reported honestly rather than assumed.
