# Ethical & Professional Standards: Relocating the Site of Ethical Obligation

*Companion to [`docs/theoretical-framework.md`](theoretical-framework.md) and [`docs/methodology.md`](methodology.md). This project has no human participants and no IRB-reviewed data collection; its ethical claim is about where research ethics review is conventionally assumed to begin, and why that assumption is itself a decolonial problem.*

## Primary-Source Control Statement

The primary source audited in this project is David L. Altheide's (1996) *Qualitative Media Analysis*, accessed through the SAGE Research Methods (SRM) digital platform (DOI: [10.4135/9781412985536](https://doi.org/10.4135/9781412985536)). This project cites that digital edition using SRM chapter titles and SRM PDF page numbers. Earlier draft materials that referenced a 2013 print edition (Altheide & Schneider) were revised, because that edition's pagination could not be verified against the digital source actually used in this audit and the 2013 edition is a different, co-authored text. No unverified print-page crosswalk is presented as a final citation.

## 1. The Standard Model, and Where It Starts Too Late

Research-ethics training; IRB protocols, informed consent, confidentiality; locates the ethical moment *after* a topic and a method have already been chosen: once a researcher has selected a canon and a design, ethics governs how participants are treated inside that design. This project argues that framing arrives too late. By the time a researcher opens a manual like Altheide's (1996) *Qualitative Media Analysis* and begins designing a study, the canon has already determined what counts as legitimate data, evidence, and method; which archives are searchable, which testimonies are admissible, which categories are available to code into. That determination is rarely treated as an ethical choice, because it is packaged as a *technical* one: which software, which database, which protocol template. Santos's sociology of absences (`[REF-SAN-2014]`), Grosfoguel's genealogy of epistemicide (`[REF-GRO-2013]`), and polanco et al.'s account of methodological extraction (`[REF-POL-2020]`) are not, at bottom, purely descriptive theories of knowledge. They are arguments that harm has already occurred by the time a study is designed; in the formation of the canon a researcher is trained to treat as neutral. Patin et al. (2021) make this explicit for information professionals specifically, proposing a "practical framework for naming, identifying, and ending epistemic injustice" that locates ethical responsibility in classification and infrastructure decisions made long before any individual research project begins (`[REF-PAT-2021]`).

## 2. Why This Counts as Ethics, Not Only Epistemology

This project does not assert a new "should" on top of these theorists' work. It names what their arguments already are: a professional-ethics claim about harm reduction *prior* to study design, addressed to the people who build and maintain canons and infrastructure; textbook authors, methods instructors, database vendors, catalogers, information architects; rather than to individual researchers managing individual participants. This is consistent with existing professional-ethics literature in information science: Olson (2002) and Berman (1971) treat classification-scheme design itself as a site of ethical consequence, not a downstream technical detail (`[REF-OLS-2002]`, `[REF-BER-1971]`); Duarte and Belarde-Lewis (2015) and Sutherland (2021) frame the redesign of knowledge-organization systems as an act of professional and ethical obligation, not optional advocacy (`[REF-DUA-2015]`, `[REF-SUT-2021]`). The move this project makes is to apply that same standard reflexively to a qualitative-methods textbook, arguing that "which indexing software the manual recommends" is exactly the kind of pre-design decision this literature says should be subject to ethical scrutiny.

## 3. Applying the Standard to This Project's Own Conduct

Two concrete choices in this project's own method are the direct, practiced application of that standard, not just its citation:

- **Bounded reconstruction (see `docs/methodology.md`, "Looking Ahead").** Where a tagged absence would require reconstructing a suppressed knowledge tradition the researcher has no standing in; Arab-Islamic scientific thought, Indigenous calendrical systems, oral archives lost to genocide; this project marks the unit *unresolved* rather than inventing a resolution. This is a direct, practical application of "harm reduction before design": refusing to speak for a tradition is treated as more ethical than a confident but unearned reconstruction, distinguishing this work from what Saidiya Hartman (2008) calls "critical fabulation"; a deliberate, disclosed act of imaginative narration used to speak into an archive's silences (`[REF-HAR-2008]`). This project does not attempt that move; it marks the gap and stops.
- **Disclosed positionality as a condition of coding, not an afterword (`reflexive_log` on every record, `sage-audit-data-final.json`).** Each coding decision discloses the specific institutional privilege that made the coding possible in the first place; proxy access to LEXIS/NEXIS, training on NVivo, standard social-science methods socialization; rather than presenting the audit's conclusions from an unlocated, "zero-point" vantage the project is simultaneously critiquing (`[REF-CAS-2010]`).

## 4. The Professional Implication

If the ethical failure this project identifies happens upstream of study design, the professional response has to happen upstream too. The implication for IT and information-science practice is not "treat research participants more carefully"; it is "audit the canon and the infrastructure *before* they are handed to the next researcher, student, or system." Concretely: methods instructors auditing which manuals they assign, database vendors and catalogers auditing which indexing and classification defaults they ship, and information architects auditing which schema decisions get made before any data is collected. This project's six-step protocol (`docs/methodology.md`) is offered as one reproducible way to do that auditing; a professional practice aimed at the pre-design stage, rather than a compliance checklist applied once a study is already underway.

## 5. Naming the Mechanism in the Project's Own Method

Ethical disclosure under this standard cannot stop at the object of audit; it has to include the audit's own apparatus. This project's data structure performs the same technical operation polanco et al. (2020) name as "vacuum-packing" (*empacadas al vacío*) and "data-ization": literal textbook excerpts are isolated from their surrounding context and converted into discrete, coded, machine-searchable JSON fields (`[REF-POL-2020]`). The mechanism is structurally identical to what the term describes. The object is not: polanco et al.'s critique addresses the extraction of vernacular, embodied testimony from marginalized narrators for academic prestige, whereas this project applies the same mechanism to a canonical, professionally published SAGE manual. Naming the mechanical parallel without collapsing the two cases; same operation, different object, different stakes; is itself an application of Wolcott's (2009) transparency standard to the audit's own method, not only to the text it audits (`[REF-WOL-2009]`). A fuller accounting of where this parallel holds, and where it deliberately does not extend, is in [`docs/reflexive-self-audit.md`](reflexive-self-audit.md).
